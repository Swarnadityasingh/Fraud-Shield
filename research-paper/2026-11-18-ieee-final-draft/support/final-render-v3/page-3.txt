FraudShield uses an empirical validation grid and does not 
estimate business-optimal thresholds. 
Calibration is a separate property from discrimination. 
Carriero et al. investigate imbalance corrections and risk 
calibration in a clinical/simulation setting [12], while Lin et al. 
examine posterior -estimation effects of balancing strategies 
[13]. Matsubara et al. propose calibration assessment designed 
for imbalanced settings [14]. These studies justify stating 
calibration as an unresolved evaluation question. They do not 
prove that FraudShield is either calibrated or miscalibrated, and 
no correction is applied here. 
D. Financial explanations and study position 
The financial explanation studies above also expose a 
difference in explanatory scope. Local attribution, prototype 
reasoning and interpretable additive structures ask different 
questions from ranking a forest's global split importance [8], [9], 
[11]. Frau dShield's impurity rankings can describe aggregate 
fitted-model relevance but cannot be used as evidence that an 
individual decision has been explained. The same separation 
applies to calibration: work on balancing and posterior 
estimation motivates a miss ing evaluation dimension, not an 
explanation for the observed fraud reversal [12], [13], [14]. 
The resulting positioning is a bounded empirical study with 
supporting implementation documentation. It adds an explicitly 
reported candidate/ threshold comparison and an unfavorable 
selection-stability observation for these data; it does not 
introduce an algorithm or claim a field -wide methodological 
gap. This targeted literature synthesis identifies protocol 
differences and unresolved comparison details rather than 
constructing a cross-paper leaderboard or claiming a systematic 
review. Bibliographic verification is not independent validation 
of the cited experiments. [E08–E10, E22] 
III. BOUNDED RESEARCH GAP, AIM AND QUESTIONS 
A. Project-specific research gap 
Fraud detection and borrower delinquency are established 
imbalanced classification tasks, with existing weighting, 
threshold-selection, calibration and explanation methods. The 
unresolved empirical question within this implementation is 
how the chosen fore st candidates behave at validation-selected 
operating points and whether their validation ordering persists 
on the designated holdouts. The project addresses that question 
through preserved comparisons, retaining the unfavorable fraud 
reversal rather than changing selection after seeing test results. 
This is a contextual empirical contribution, not evidence that the 
field lacks these methods. [E08, E09, E22] 
Artifact/source traceability and global rankings provide 
supporting context but do not complete the pending application-
verification question. Class imbalance remains partially 
addressed; exact historical weighted -fraud fit rows remain 
unproven; global re levance is not local explanation; shared 
application structure is not fused prediction; and no drift detector 
or adaptation is demonstrated. [E01–E10, E15–E17, E20] 
B. Research aim and current-study objectives 
The research aim is to evaluate and document two 
independent Random Forest pipelines for transaction fraud 
and serious-delinquency prediction using explicit validation-
based selection and preserved holdout evidence, and to 
document their source -level integration within an 
authenticated application while separating prior checks 
from prospective live verification.

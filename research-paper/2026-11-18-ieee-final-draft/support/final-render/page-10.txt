VIII. APPLICATION IMPLEMENTATION AND DEMONSTRATION 
BOUNDARY 
A. Source implementation and previously documented checks 
The active fraud form accepts a complete 431 -key JSON 
object, and the credit form renders ten numeric inputs. The 
backend validates schema/ values, applies the appropriate 
pipeline and commits a result owned by the authenticated user. 
Its response contains class, score, threshold, model hash, display 
risk, history ID and time. History -list and individual retrieval 
enforce ownership. These facts are supported by source and 
saved checks rather than by newly manufactured browser 
outcomes. [E01–E06, E11] 
The UI displays scores as percentages and labels them Low 
below 0.30, Medium from 0.30 to below 0.70, and High at or 
above 0.70. The display bands are independent of classification 
thresholds and have not been validated as risk categories. A 
positive classification with a Low band is possible by design. 
Global importance is not returned as a per -case explanation. 
[E02, E04] 
Dashboard and Analytics aggregate loaded owned history. 
Reports filters by model, creates CSV client -side and invokes 
the browser print dialog. Source implementation does not prove 
that downloaded bytes or printed layout are correct. The history 
table has no individual -detail action despite the API endpoint, 
and file management remains a placeholder. [E02–E04] 
The prior audit documents a PostgreSQL migration/

Abstract—Financial fraud detection and borrower 
delinquency assessment involve different targets but share the 
need for careful evaluation under class imbalance. FraudShield 
integrates two independent Random Forest pipelines within an 
authenticated applica tion. This study reports preserved 
experiments comparing weighted and unweighted forests with a 
constant-prior baseline. IEEE -CIS uses chronological 70/ 15/15 
partitions, while Give Me Some Credit uses stratified random 
partitions in the same proportions. Forests and decision thresholds 
are selected using validation F1. The selected unweighted models 
use 431 and 10 inputs, with thresholds of 0.21 and 0.23. Saved 
holdout results give fraud precision 0.343373, recall 0.406747 and 
F1 0.372383 on 88,581 transa ctions; credit precision is 0.407090, 
recall 0.473404 and F1 0.437750 on 22,500 borrowers. The 
unweighted fraud forest wins validation F1, but its weighted 
comparator achieves higher holdout F1, 0.444087. This reversal is 
retained rather than used for retr ospective model selection. 
Artifact inspection supports common fitted preprocessing, 
although exact historical training -row identity for the reused 
weighted fraud model remains unproven. Trapezoidal precision–
recall area and Average Precision are reported separately. The 
empirical contribution is a task-specific candidate-and-operating-
point comparison that exposes a validation -to-holdout ordering 
reversal without test-based reselection. Application integration is 
documented at source level; live end -to-end verification is a 
prospective systems question, outside the answered research 
questions. The study does not establish a new detection method, 
calibrated financial risk, fused prediction or drift adaptation.

I. INTRODUCTION AND RESEARCH PROBLEM 
Financial applications may present transaction monitoring 
and borrower assessment within one interface, but integration 
does not make their prediction targets equivalent. A transaction-
fraud label concerns a transaction, whereas the Give Me Some 
Credit tar get concerns a borrower outcome. FraudShield 
therefore uses separate feature schemas, fitted preprocessors, 
classifiers and decision thresholds. The system shares 
authentication, persistence and reporting rather than learning a 
combined financial -risk scor e. Its actual page wiring and 
inference routes support this distinction. [E01–E07] 
Imbalanced outcomes make accuracy alone insufficient for 
understanding positive-case detection. Precision measures how 
often a generated positive prediction corresponds to a positive 
label; recall measures coverage of actual positives. Their 
operating point depends on a threshold. Ranking measures 
provide complementary information, but do not establish 
probability calibration or institutional utility [1], [2], [3]. The 
present problem is consequently to characterize the operating 
behavior of two task -specific pipelines while retaining the 
distinction between training, validation selection, holdout 
assessment and application operation. 
Random Forests provide an established approach to tabular 
classification [4]. Their use does not itself constitute algorithmic 
novelty. Similarly, a dashboard does not establish that model 
outputs are reliable in lending or fraud -investigation practice. 
Credit-scoring benchmarks illustrate the breadth of comparisons 
needed for algorithm -superiority claims [5], and operational 
fraud research highlights temporal change and delayed labels 
[6]. FraudShield has a narrower scope: two weighting choices 
within one forest configuration, one prior baseline and one 
internal holdout protocol per task. 
The empirical contribution has two parts. First, the study 
reports how specified weighting candidates behave at their own 
validation-selected operating points under two different 
internal-holdout designs. Second, it documents a selection 
reversal: the unwe ighted fraud candidate's validation F1 
advantage does not persist on the later holdout. Retaining the 
original selection makes this an account of selection stability 
rather than an optimistic report of the best test result. Global 
feature rankings and arti fact/source traceability support 
interpretation and implementation documentation; they are not 
new explanation methods or completed live -system evidence.

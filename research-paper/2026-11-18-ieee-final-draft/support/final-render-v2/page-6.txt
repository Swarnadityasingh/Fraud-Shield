B. Validation selection and decision rules 
For each of three candidates, thresholds 0.01 –0.99 in 
increments of 0.01 are examined on validation scores. 
Maximum F1 selects the candidate's operating point, with ties 
resolved by higher precision and then higher threshold. Only the 
two forests compete for selection using their best validation F1. 
The prior classifier receives its own threshold for comparison 
but cannot become the selected serving model. There are 297 
saved validation operating points per dataset. [E08, E09] 
The selected thresholds are 0.21 for fraud and 0.23 for credit. 
A score s(x) is positive when s(x) ≥ t. Current code implements 
validation-based selection, but neither the code nor present 
outputs independently certify the full chronology of historical 
analyst decisions or absence of holdout -guided choices. F1 
selection is not a measured financial -cost optimization. [E04, 
E08, E15] [3] 
C. Measures and their interpretation 
For class 1, precision is TP/ (TP+FP), recall TP/ (TP+FN), 
and F1 2PR/ (P+R). Accuracy is (TP+TN)/ N. ROC -AUC 
summarizes ranking over positive/negative pairs. The evaluator 
computes trapezoidal PR -AUC using auc(recall, precision) on 
the precision–recall c urve and computes Average Precision 
separately using average_precision_score. AP weights precision 
by recall increments; the two measures must remain distinct. 
[E08] [1], [2] 
Earlier validation JSONs used pr_ auc as the name for AP. 
Those legacy values are not substituted into final -result tables. 
Constant-score baseline curves also require caution: trapezoidal 
interpolation can yield an apparently large area without useful 
ranking. Both stored quantities are reta ined faithfully, rather 
than retrospectively redefining a metric. [E09, E12] [2]
